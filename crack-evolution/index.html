THE PROBLEM

Beyond a crack mask

Is this the same crack as before? Which part has just appeared?

Crack propagation provides richer information than static observations for assessing concrete infrastructure. Yet repeated images differ in both acquisition time and camera viewpoint: apparent changes caused by camera motion must be separated from actual crack growth.

This study formulates visual crack-growth monitoring as a cross-view and cross-time crack-instance tracking task. A geometry-assisted, morphology-aware framework aligns observations in a shared reference view, organizes segmented pixels into crack instances, and propagates their identities while extracting newly developed extension. A temporally annotated dataset covers single dominant cracks, mixed-mode branching, and multiple-crack interaction.

The experiments demonstrate loose cross-view identity correspondence under changing viewpoints. Strict spatial overlap and growth localization remain limited by early micro-crack segmentability and projection drift.

METHOD

Align. Identify. Follow the growth.

Geometric estimation and image segmentation provide the foundation. Morphology-aware instantiation and temporal association turn frame-wise masks into crack histories.

01 / GEOMETRY & SEGMENTATION

Bring observations into a shared view

Pretrained MASt3R estimates dense geometry and camera poses. Adjacent-pair mappings project earlier crack observations into the coordinate frame of the later image.

A fixed DeepLabv3+ checkpoint supplies crack masks, without training or fine-tuning on these temporal scenes.

02 / MORPHOLOGY-AWARE INSTANTIATION

Organize crack pixels into instances

Skeleton branches and their dominant directions guide crack separation and continuity-based merging.

Constrained grouping reconnects compatible fragments while preserving hard-separation constraints between incompatible branches.

03 / TEMPORAL ASSOCIATION

Attribute new extension to the same crack

Projected instances are matched and their identities propagated through successive observations.

Aligned skeletons separate inherited support from newly appeared extension. Growth length is evaluated in image coordinates, reducing the influence of mask-width changes.

Immediate Growth F1 is a growth-length-weighted, instance-level detection diagnostic. Growth skeletons of at most 1 px are excluded. A growth skeleton is matched when at least one counterpart in the same scene and interval has a bidirectional support-band F1 of at least 0.10, using a 16 px radius. Each matched skeleton contributes its full length once on its own side; matching is not constrained to be one-to-one. The full method has precision 0.759, recall 0.624, and F1 0.685.

Reference-growth coverage across observation intervals

Delayed Growth Recall

DGR@K measures the growth-length-weighted reference-side coverage obtained from the earliest one-to-one match in the original interval or within the next K pair intervals. It counts the covered fraction of each reference growth skeleton, and each prediction can be assigned only once.

The values are 0.303, 0.313, 0.317, and 0.319 for K = 0, 1, 2, and 3. The increase from K = 0 to K = 2 is 1.4 percentage points: allowing two additional intervals provides limited extra reference-growth coverage under the adopted assignment rule and available observations.

K counts observation intervals, not elapsed physical time. Immediate Growth Recall counts the full length of matched reference skeletons; DGR@0 counts only their covered fractions under one-to-one matching.

TEMPORAL CRACK DATASET

Staged loading. Changing viewpoints.

Handheld images were acquired from eight independent concrete specimens during staged loading. The camera was repositioned between observations, retaining overlap around the crack region.

Single dominant propagation

Mode I opening cracks in pre-notched beams extend along a dominant path.

Turning and branching

Mixed-mode flexural–shear cracking in L-shaped beams produces turning and branching near a re-entrant corner.

Multiple-crack interaction

Mode II-dominated diagonal shear cracks in deep beams emerge, propagate, and interact within the same observation.

Pixel-level masks annotate visible cracks; scene-local labels maintain physical crack identity across loading stages. Geometry-assisted, manually reviewed growth references provide the common evaluation basis. The fixed segmentation checkpoint was not trained or fine-tuned on these eight specimens.

Scope and current limitations

Validation is limited to eight laboratory scenes, one fixed segmentation checkpoint, and fixed pixel-scale tolerances; multi-instance discrimination is primarily evaluated in Scenes 6–8. Missing micro-crack support and projection drift affect overlap, identity continuity, and growth measurement. Field generalization, scale transfer, irregular inspection intervals, and alternative segmentation models remain unverified. Converting image-space growth to millimeters requires calibrated scale information. The manually reviewed reference supports consistent comparison but depends on the available projections.
